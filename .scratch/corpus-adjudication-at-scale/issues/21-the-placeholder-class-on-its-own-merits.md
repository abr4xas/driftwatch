# 21: The placeholder class, weighed without looking at condition 6

**What to decide:** whether `#anchor-a`, `#anchor-b` and `+types/` are a discard rule worth
having — judged on what it costs over 700 repositories, and **not** on what it does to a bar.

**Type:** task

**Blocked by:** nothing

**Status: resolved 2026-09-19.** Two questions, two answers: `+types/` yes, the anchors no.
See §"Answer".

## Why this ticket exists separately

Ticket `20` found condition 6 at 87.9%, and closing this class is one of the two moves that
would restore it. That is precisely why the decision is taken here instead: a rule derived to
move a number is the mistake the criterion exists to prevent.

So this ticket is written to be decidable **without** condition 6 in the frame. If the rule
earns its place the bar may recover as a side effect, and nobody will have tuned to the test.
If it does not, it does not, and `20` stands as recorded.

## The findings

Three, in two calibration repositories, ruled false in round eighteen as class D:

| repository | claim | check |
|---|---|---|
| `vercel/next.js` | `#anchor-a` | `link/broken` |
| `vercel/next.js` | `#anchor-b` | `link/broken` |
| `remix-run/react-router` | `+types/` | `path/missing` |

Round eighteen's own note on the third: *"class D, generated by typegen"*.

## The case for a rule

`discard.ts` already refuses the placeholder convention in four shapes — `NNNN`, `XXXX` and
`ID` as uppercase stand-ins, `iterN` as a trailing-capital index, `vX.Y.Z` and `YYYY-MM-DD` as
templates, `path/to` as a metasyntactic sequence. Each entry carries the real document it came
from.

`#anchor-a` and `#anchor-b` are the same convention in a grammar the extractor has not been
told about: a lettered stand-in inside an anchor, where the document is describing the *shape*
of a link rather than naming one. The gap is that `links.ts` filters synthesised anchors
(`#top`, `#readme`) and line references (`#L12`) and nothing else.

## The case against, and it is two separate cases

**`+types/` is not a placeholder.** It is a real directory that React Router's typegen writes,
and the document is right that it exists — after a build. The class that describes it is
*generated*, which is what `HEDGED`'s `is generated` / `are generated` markers are for and
what `generated.ts` already reasons about. Filing it under placeholders because it arrived in
the same round is how a taxonomy rots, and a rule built to cover both would be built to cover
a coincidence.

**A lettered anchor is a thin signal.** `#anchor-a` is obviously a stand-in to a reader.
`#step-a`, `#option-b`, `#appendix-c` are not — they are ordinary anchors in ordinary
documents. Any rule narrow enough to be safe may be narrow enough to be about two findings in
one repository, which is a rule fitted to a document rather than to a convention.

## What to do

1. Decide whether these are **one class or two**. The answer is probably two, and if so this
   ticket is about the anchors alone; `+types/` belongs with the generated-file reasoning and
   may already be reachable there.
2. Propose the narrowest rule that covers the anchors and say what real documents it came
   from — the convention every entry in `discard.ts` follows.
3. **Price it the way ticket `16` established**: the certification corpus before and after,
   then the 700 discovery repositories audited twice with the findings diffed. `16` is the
   precedent for why the first measurement alone proves nothing — the 66 did not contain that
   shape either.
4. Only then look at what it did to condition 6, and record that as a consequence rather than
   as a justification.

## What would make the answer "no"

A rule that suppresses any true finding in the discovery diff. A rule whose only real case is
`vercel/next.js`. A rule that has to reach `+types/` to be worth writing. Any of those and the
class stays open and the ticket says so — which is a fine outcome, and the one `20` is already
written for.

## Answer

Resolved 2026-09-19. **They were two classes and they got two answers.** `+types/` is
generated and is now suppressed; the anchors are not a class and the ecosystem said so.
Round twenty-six. Condition 6 is unmoved, which is the point.

### `+types/` — yes, and it is one word

Not a placeholder. React Router's typegen writes it beside every route module, and the skill
names it the plainest way there is: *"imports from `./+types/...`"*. Nothing tracks it — not in
`remix-run/react-router`, not in any of the three discovery repositories that mention it.

So it is `GENERATED`'s category, sitting with `.react-router`, `.next`, `.nuxt`, `.svelte-kit`
and `.astro`, which are the same thing for other frameworks. One entry.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| corpus | 38 findings, 11 false | **37, 10 false** |
| 700 discovery repositories | 1407 findings | **1407** |

One false positive removed and nothing else moved, anywhere.

### The anchors — no, and this is the half worth having

§"The case against" guessed that a rule narrow enough to be safe might be narrow enough to be
about one repository. The discovery corpus was more definite than that: **both candidate rules
are wrong, not merely thin.**

| rule | what it would suppress |
|---|---|
| an anchor beginning with `anchor` | 8 repositories write one, **7 mean it** — `#anchor-versions`, `#anchor-system`, `#anchormanager`, `#anchorbasedwriter` |
| `word-<single character>` | **57 distinct links in 15 repositories** — `#layer-1`, `#item-5`, `#state-1`, `#quote-s` |

Numbered and lettered headings are how documents number and letter their headings. Either rule
trades dozens of real, checkable anchors for two stand-ins in one repository, which is the
trade this project exists to refuse.

What makes `#anchor-a` obviously a placeholder to a reader is that the **whole sentence** is a
specimen of output the skill is telling an agent to write. That is a property of the
surrounding prose, not of the anchor, and nothing in `links.ts` reads prose. It is not
unreachable — the prose gates do exactly this for path claims — but it is a different piece of
work and no finding in either corpus is asking for it.

### Condition 6, looked at last and on purpose

`remix-run/react-router` keeps `app/entry.server.tsx`, so it is still unquiet.
`vercel/next.js` keeps both anchors. **58 of 66 = 87.9%**, unchanged.

The justified change did not move the bar and the change that would move it is not justified.
That is the ticket working as designed: had these been weighed together with the bar in view,
the anchor rule is exactly the one somebody would have talked themselves into.

### What §"What would make the answer no" asked for

All three of its conditions were met by the anchor half — a rule whose only real case is
`vercel/next.js`, and one that would suppress true findings in the wild. The class stays open
and this ticket says so, which §"What would make the answer no" already called a fine outcome.
